**Finding 1: seed paper probably counts as "seed sold for planting".** No source found speaks about seed paper
directly. But state seed laws and the Federal Seed Act cover flower seed offered for sale for planting, and they
define "labeling" broadly, including printing on the product itself.
- **Labels:** states typically require the kind (species) and variety, the labeller's name and address, and a test
  or pack date. Virginia, for example, requires the name and address of the seller and the test or pack date.
  California requires the labeller's name and address and the kind and variety of seed.
- **Across state lines:** the Federal Seed Act also requires the kind or variety, and the shipper's name and address
  (or a USDA-approved code), on seed shipped across state lines.
- **Licences or registration:** some states want seed sellers registered, even sellers based out of state:
  - Florida: seed dealer registration, $10 under $500 in sales.
  - Pennsylvania: a seed licence for the name on the label.
  - Minnesota: a permit for the initial labeller, even out of state.
  - California: registration for agricultural and vegetable seed sellers.
- **Who is the labeller?** plantableseedpaper.com sells the blank paper. When we cut it into swatches and sell it
  in our envelope, we may become the labeller. Open: ask the vendor what labelling and compliance they cover.

**Finding 2: each state bans its own list of weed seeds.** Sellers who ship across state lines must follow the list
of the state they ship into. USDA publishes the combined list: "State Noxious-Weed Seed Requirements" (2026 edition).
Oxeye daisy and Queen Anne's lace are among the most often banned wildflowers; neither is in our mix.

**Finding 3: what's in Ken's wildflower paper.** The vendor's Oats and Canary Wildflower pages say the mix "may
include" black-eyed Susan, mountain garland (Clarkia), baby's breath, spurred snapdragon, sweet William, bird's eye
and corn poppy, depending on availability.
- Check each species against the USDA state list before choosing where we ship. **Baby's breath (Gypsophila
  paniculata)** is the one I'd check first; I believe some western states list it as a noxious weed (unverified).
- Pets: baby's breath, sweet William (Dianthus) and poppies appear on common lists of plants toxic to cats and dogs
  (to verify against the ASPCA list).
- A University of Washington study of 19 wildflower seed packets found invasive species in every one, and
  8 held seeds of a plant listed as a noxious weed somewhere in the US or Canada.
- **Special cases:** Hawaii requires permits for most incoming seed. California inspects incoming plant material.
  Proposed: leave Hawaii out at first.

**Finding 5: Texas (home state).** Texas seed law is Agriculture Code chapter 61 (Texas Department of Agriculture).
- Seed labellers pay a fee of $0.45 per 100 lb of seed sold, by buying "Texas Tested Seed Labels" or holding a
  quarterly reporting permit. Retailers selling others' labelled seed need no permit.
- Vegetable seed needs a $350 licence; nothing found says flower seed does.
- TxDOT says wildflower seed must meet the Texas Seed Law, including labelling (pure live seed, name and type of seed).
- A seller guide says Texas requires a germination test within 9 months before sale (Agriculture Code 61.009).
- Our seed weight is tiny (grams per set), so any per-pound fee would be cents. The real question is whether seed
  paper counts as "seed" under chapter 61 at all. Nothing found says so either way.
- **Proposed:** one phone call settles it: TDA Seed Quality Office, Giddings, (979) 542-3691. Ask: "Is plantable
  seed paper with wildflower seed, sold in a gift card set, covered by the Texas Seed Law, and what do I need?"
